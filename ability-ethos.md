# Playa Court ability ethos

A working guide for writing card abilities. Edit freely; this is a draft.

## 1. Plus before minus
Abilities add power. Minuses are rare (about 1 card in 10), small (−1 or −2), and only on Queens or Jokers. If a plus wording does nearly the same job, use it: "Aura: Your camp gets +1 for each [Dance] in any camp" instead of "Aura: Enemy [Dance] get −1".

## 2. Tags drive abilities
Every ability names a tag, counts tags, or keys off a class. See **Tag rules** below for how a card gets its tags.
- **Allies**: cards in your camp.
- **Enemies**: cards in your opponent's camp.
- **Rogues**: cards with no camp (no camp badge). Abilities can count, boost or target Rogues just like a tag: "Aura: Your Rogues get +1", "Self: +2 for each Rogue in any camp", "+3 against a Rogue".

**Camps:** leave the camp blank, making the card a Rogue, when the answer isn't a real camp:
- staff, staff camping, volunteer or crew areas
- the person's own name (Stu → Stu)
- none, wherever, open camping, and similar answers

## 3. Class roles
| Class | Caption | What its abilities do |
|---|---|---|
| King | rallies their allies | Build-around. Boosts **your** cards with a tag. Never touches the enemy. |
| Queen | manipulates the battlefield | Turns tags on **either** side into power for your camp. Where a minus exists, it lives here. |
| Knight | champions a quest | Gains power from one **specific enemy tag**. Its quest is that tag. |
| Page | learns from the field | Scales with the **variety** of tags on both sides. The biggest ceiling, but needs a varied board. |
| Joker | wild and independent | Odd, situational power spikes: big bonuses against a specific class, a Rogue, or under a strange condition. Works within the normal rules; the rare game-changing abilities mostly live here. |

The sign-up form describes the classes as: King *strategic, experienced, plan-focused*; Queen *supportive, balanced, community-focused*; Knight *movement, change, goal-focused*; Page *passionate, enthusiastic, learning-focused*; Joker *independent, wild, insight-focused*.

## 4. Size
- **Ongoing** (no label): +1 or +2 to a tag, or +1 per count.
- **This round:** up to +3 flat, or +1 to +2 per count.
- **Pages** can scale highest, but only off a varied board.
- **Jokers** sit at power 1–5. A conditional Joker bonus can be big (+5 or +6) because it only fires in one matchup.

## 5. Jokers
Don't try to break the rules. A Joker already beats Kings; a good Joker ability gives it a second, different way to win, like "Self: +6 against a Knight", so it nearly always beats two classes and is a gamble against the rest. Keep it to one clear condition.

**What's common and what's rare:**
- **Common, for any class:** Burn Out, Revive and Loud. They're part of the normal game.
- **Rare, about 1 card in 15:** abilities that change how a round is decided, like "Beats don't count this round", reversing the ring, or swapping played cards. They can exist, but they're confusing, so keep them few, and mostly on Jokers.

## 6. Labels and keywords
- **Scope labels** (new, being trialled on the first submissions):
  - **Self:** affects only this card.
  - **Aura:** affects other cards too, for as long as this card is in its camp.
- Timing labels: **This round:**, **If this wins:**, **If this loses:**.
- Keywords: **Burn Out**, **Revive**, **Loud**. (Legend is retired.)

## 7. Writing
- One ability per card, at most 135 characters (4 lines).
- Plain words. Keywords get reminder text in parentheses.
- **Leave out "card" where it's implied**: "All [Food] get +2", not "All [Food] cards get +2".
- **Don't assume two players.** The rules stay 1-on-1 for now, but word abilities so they'd still work in a 3–4 player match:
  - "any camp" or "all", not "either camp" or "both camps"
  - "your" for your side, "opponents'" or "enemy" for everyone else, not "your opponent's"

## 8. Fit to the person
The ability should read like what that person is known for; the tags and flavor line back it up.

## 9. Tag rules
A card gets **one or two** tags, read from the sign-up form.

**Sources, in order:**
1. The three features, taking the most concrete first.
2. The camp's purpose, if it's clear from its name (a store, a kitchen, a sound camp).
3. Nothing else. Don't tag the role, a vibe, or what's implied.

**Rules:**
- **Every tag needs a source you can point to.** If you can't say which feature or camp it came from, drop it.
- **Each source gives at most one tag.** If a feature could mean two tags (poi: [Fire] or [Dance]), pick the stronger.
- **Literal beats inferred.** "Dancing with a fan" is [Dance]; "Partying" is not [Dance] unless dancing is mentioned.
- **One tag is fine.** Don't add a second one just to fill the line.
- **Feelings and skills aren't tags.** Joyful heart, lifelong learning and time dilation go in the ability or flavor, not the type line. The exception is when they clearly point to a tag, like a smile and a joyful heart pointing to [Care].
- **Substances are PG-13.** When smoking or drinking is part of who someone is, keep it: it can show in the art, flavor and ability, and lead to a nearby tag (spirit tastings → [Food]). There's no tag of its own for it.

**What each tag covers:**
| Tag | Comes from |
|---|---|
| [Food] | Cooking, serving, tastings and drinks, kitchens, commissaries, stores and snack camps |
| [Sound] | Music, instruments, DJing, singing, sound camps |
| [Dance] | Dancing (stated), fan dance, dance camps |
| [Fire] | Fire spinning, poi, fire circles, burns |
| [Glow] | Lights, LEDs, glow art, lanterns |
| [Art] | Making art, crafts, costumes, piñatas, murals |
| [Build] | Planning, fixing, building, executing, logistics |
| [Care] | Hosting, kindness, medics, welcoming, joy and smiles |
| [Crew] | Staff, volunteering, teams, getting shit done |
| [Night] | Late nights, raves, parties after dark, partying |
| [Vehicle] | Art cars, bikes, mutant vehicles |

**Worked examples:**
- **Zaddy:** smoking weed (no tag), piñata → [Art], partying → [Night], The Corn'r Store → [Food]. That gives three candidates, so keep two: the camp's purpose plus the most concrete feature, **[Food] [Art]**.
- **Wob:** poi → [Fire], The Fire Circle → [Fire] again, while time dilation and lifelong learning give no tag. Result: **[Fire]**.
- **ISHYne:** joyful heart and smile → [Care], getting shit done → [Crew]. "Staff camping" isn't a camp, so ISHYne is a Rogue. Result: **[Care] [Crew]**, no camp.

## 10. Elements and the deck (final pass)
Once every card is in, each one also gets an **element** and a **rank**, so a set of Playa Court cards works as a deck of playing cards or the minor arcana of a tarot deck. This happens **last**: the person comes first, and their class, tags and ability are written from the form as usual. Elements don't change how Playa Court plays.

**The four elements**
The classic tarot pairing:

| Element | Tarot suit | Playing-card suit | What it stands for | Tags that point to it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fire | Wands | ♣ Clubs | Passion, energy, creativity, spark. The fire spinners and night owls. | [Fire], [Glow], [Night] |
| Water | Cups | ♥ Hearts | Love, care, feelings, sharing. The hosts, cooks and huggers. | [Food], [Care] |
| Air | Swords | ♠ Spades | Mind, voice, movement, change. The DJs, dancers and drivers. | [Sound], [Dance], [Vehicle] |
| Earth | Pentacles | ♦ Diamonds | Craft, work, building, the material. The builders, crew and makers. | [Build], [Crew], [Art] |

**Ranks**
- **Number cards, 1–10:** the rank is the card's power. The 1 is the Ace.
- **Court cards:** one King, Queen, Knight and Page per element. The court rank must match the card's class. A court card's power can be anything.
- **Jokers:** Joker-class cards are rank **0**, the Fool. They are never number or court cards. A playing-card deck uses two of them.
- In a 52-card deck, the Page plays as the Jack and Knights are left out. Tarot uses all four court cards.

**The corner index**
Each card shows its element symbol and rank in two opposite corners, like a playing card, so the rank still shows when the cards are fanned. The index is separate from the power box. On a number card it repeats the power; on a court card it shows K, Q, Kn or P; on a Joker it shows 0.

**Assigning, in order**
1. **Element from tags.** Use the card's first tag. A card whose two tags point to different elements can take either one, whichever fills a gap.
2. **Courts.** In each element, pick the King, Queen, Knight and Page who best fit it, such as a fire circle lead for King of Fire.
3. **Numbers.** Fill 1–10 in each element. To fill a gap, power can move by **up to 2**. When it does, resize the ability to keep the card balanced: a higher power gets a smaller ability.
4. **Spread the classes.** Each element's ten number cards should mix Kings, Queens, Knights and Pages, not mostly one class.
5. **Gaps.** One full deck needs 58 cards: 40 numbers, 16 courts and 2 Jokers. If a slot can't be filled, leave it empty or fill it with a camp or event card.
6. **Duplicates are fine.** With more people than slots, several cards share a rank. Any one of them completes the deck.

**Power 10.** Number cards need a power 10 in each element. Give power 9 and 10 cards a small or conditional ability.
